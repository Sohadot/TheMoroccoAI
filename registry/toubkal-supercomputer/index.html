Toubkal supercomputer

Toubkal

High-performance computing system at the African Supercomputing Centre of Mohammed VI Polytechnic University, installed in 2020 and listed in the June 2026 TOP500 at rank 409, with 71,232 cores and a 3.16 PFlop/s Linpack result. It is general-purpose computing infrastructure with CPU and GPU partitions. Authors affiliated with its host university report AI workloads run on it, including the training of a genomic language model; no independent documentation of its AI use, nor its scale or users, is recorded in this registry.

  • MAI-EV-0041VERIFIED FACT

    TOUBKAL: peer-reviewed description of the system and its reported workloads

    A peer-reviewed article in The Journal of Supercomputing, by authors affiliated with Mohammed VI Polytechnic University's College of Computing, describes Toubkal's GPU partitions (NVIDIA A100 nodes from the initial 2022 deployment and H100 nodes added in 2025). It states that fine-tuning of transformer-based large language models and large-scale computational fluid dynamics simulations have been run on the system, and presents as a benchmark the fine-tuning runs that produced the GemMaroc Moroccan Darija language models, which it attributes to "Belkasmi et al." while citing the GemMaroc preprint by Skiredj et al.

    “TOUBKAL: a high-performance supercomputer powering scientific research in Africa”

    “The initial deployment in 2022 included five Dell XE8545 nodes with dual AMD EPYC 7713 CPUs and four NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs per node.”

    “In 2025, three Dell XE8640 nodes were added, each with dual Intel Xeon Platinum 8468 CPUs and four NVIDIA H100-HBM3-80GB GPUs.”

    “we examine practical workloads such as fine-tuning transformer-based large language models (LLMs) and conducting large-scale computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, both of which have been successfully run on Toubkal.”

    “we present a practical benchmark demonstrating the fine-tuning of a large language model (LLM) performed on the Toubkal supercomputer by Belkasmi et al.”

    “This work led to the creation of GemMaroc”

  • MAI-EV-0043VERIFIED FACT

    GeneLM: genomic language model trained on the Toubkal supercomputer

    A peer-reviewed article by two researchers of Mohammed VI Polytechnic University's College of Computing reports that the coding-sequence classification experiments for their bacterial genomic language model were conducted on the Toubkal supercomputer, with training on two NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs for a total of 37 hours over two epochs.

    “The CDS classification experiments were conducted on the Toubkal supercomputer.”

    “The training was performed on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs, each equipped with 80GB of memory.”

    “the total training duration was 37 h for two epochs.”

    “providing access to the supercomputing resources (cc.um6p.ma/toubkal-super-computer)”

  • MAI-EV-0042VERIFIED FACT

    GemMaroc: the preprint describing the GemMaroc fine-tuning runs

    The preprint introducing the GemMaroc Moroccan Darija language models, by three authors from Mohammed VI Polytechnic University's College of Computing, one from Hugging Face and one from the National Institute of Posts and Telecoms, reports that its fine-tuning experiments ran on two NVIDIA A100-80GB GPUs, and the 27-billion-parameter GemMaroc model on eight NVIDIA H100-80GB GPUs, taking about 48 GPU-hours. It expresses the total cost as a cloud cost, using a public on-demand rate in a Runpod Cloud region, and does not name the computing facility used.

    “College of Computing, Mohammed VI Polytechnic University, Benguerir, Morocco”

    “All fine-tuning experiments were conducted on 2×A100-80GB GPUs, except for our largest model, the 27-billion-parameter GEMMAROC, which was fine-tuned on 8×H100-80GB GPUs.”

    “consuming approximately 48 GPU-hours and keeping the total cloud cost well under $100.”

    “Based on the public on-demand rate of $2 per A100-hour in the selected Runpod Cloud region.”

    Source
    arXiv (preprint by Skiredj, Azhari, Atou, Tazi and Berrada) · Academic preprint
    Published

    Retrieved
    19 October 2026
    Verification
    Verified against source

    arXiv preprint, version 1 of 20 May 2025, not peer-reviewed; excerpts matched against the PDF. The excerpt on the hardware was checked against the rendered page: the PDF's text layer splits the small-capital model name and drops the hyphen in "H100-80GB" at a line break, so automated matching reports it missing. No author is named Belkasmi: the Toubkal article (MAI-EV-0041) attributes these runs to "Belkasmi et al." but its reference [57] is this preprint by Skiredj et al. The preprint does not mention Toubkal, the African Supercomputing Centre or any acknowledgement of computing resources; its cost footnote uses a commercial cloud provider's public rate, which may be a costing convention rather than where the runs took place. It therefore neither confirms nor rules out that the runs were made on Toubkal. Three of the five authors are affiliated with the university that runs Toubkal.
